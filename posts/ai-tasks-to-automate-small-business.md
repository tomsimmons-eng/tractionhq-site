You don't need a big AI strategy to start saving time. The best first projects are repetitive, text-heavy tasks where a person still reviews the result. Here are ten we see work well for small businesses, roughly in order of how easy they are to start.

## 1. Drafting routine emails

Follow-ups, appointment reminders, "thanks for your business" notes, and replies to common questions. Give the AI a few examples of emails you've sent so it matches your tone. Someone still reads each one before it goes out.

## 2. Summarizing meetings and calls

Many video meeting and phone tools can now record (with consent) and produce summaries with action items. That saves note-taking time and keeps everyone working from the same list.

## 3. Turning notes into quotes and proposals

Feed in your site-visit notes and a proposal template, and get a clean first draft. This is often where owners win back the most evening hours.

## 4. Answering internal questions

"What's the warranty on this product?" "How do we handle a return?" An assistant connected to your own documents can answer staff questions instantly instead of interrupting the owner.

## 5. Writing job descriptions and interview questions

AI is good at turning a rough list of duties into a clear job posting and a structured set of interview questions. Review it carefully for accuracy and fairness.

## 6. Social media and marketing first drafts

Generate post ideas and drafts from your own photos, jobs, and FAQs. Keep a human editor, and don't publish anything you wouldn't say to a customer's face.

## 7. Sorting and routing incoming requests

Classify web form and email inquiries by type and urgency, pull out key details, and send them to the right person. Faster response times often mean more booked work.

## 8. Cleaning up spreadsheets and data

Ask AI to explain a formula, fix inconsistent formatting, split names and addresses into columns, or spot duplicates. Always keep a backup of the original.

## 9. Creating checklists and procedures

Describe how a task is done (or record yourself doing it) and have AI turn it into a step-by-step checklist. This is the start of documenting your processes, which pays off when you hire or eventually sell.

## 10. Reviewing documents for gaps

Paste in a contract, policy, or proposal and ask what's unclear or missing. It's not legal advice, but it's a useful second look before a professional review.

## Ground rules before you start

- **Use business accounts.** Have employees use company-managed AI accounts, not personal ones, so you control settings and data.
- **Keep sensitive data out** unless the tool is approved for it. Write this down in a short [AI use policy](/blog/employee-ai-use-policy/).
- **A person approves everything** that goes to a customer.
- **Measure it.** Before you start, note roughly how long the task takes today. Check again after a month.

## Pick one and start

Choose the task that eats the most time each week and try it for two weeks. If it saves time, roll it out to the team. If not, try the next one.

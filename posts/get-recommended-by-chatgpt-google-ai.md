More customers now skip the list of blue links and simply ask an AI assistant: "Who's a good bookkeeper near Matthews?" or "Best HVAC company in south Charlotte?" The assistant answers with a handful of names. If yours isn't one of them, you never get the chance to compete.

Getting into those answers is called **generative engine optimization (GEO)**. It builds on traditional SEO, but the emphasis shifts toward clear facts, consistency, and being easy to cite.

## How AI assistants find local businesses

There's no single database behind every assistant, but the pattern is similar:

- **Web search.** ChatGPT search, Copilot, and Perplexity run web searches behind the scenes and read the pages that come back. Google's AI features draw on Google's own index and its business data.
- **Business listings.** Google Business Profile, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, and major directories supply names, categories, hours, and reviews.
- **Your own website.** If an assistant's crawler can read your site, it can quote your services, prices, and service area directly.
- **Claimed profiles.** OpenAI now lets eligible businesses claim and manage their information in ChatGPT through its Business Hub ([OpenAI help article](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001635-setting-up-your-business-on-chatgpt)).

## A 15-minute self-check

Run these checks today. Each "no" is a fix.

1. **Ask the assistants.** Ask ChatGPT, Google, and Copilot for "best [your service] in [your town]." Note who gets named and which websites are cited as sources.
2. **Google Business Profile.** Is it claimed, verified, and complete, with the right primary category, services, service area, hours, photos, and a real description?
3. **Bing Places.** Have you claimed your listing at [Bing Places for Business](https://www.bing.com/forbusiness/)? Bing results feed Copilot and are widely used by other AI search tools.
4. **Apple Business Connect.** Is your business correct in Apple Maps via [Apple Business Connect](https://business.apple.com/)?
5. **Consistency.** Is your business name, website, phone, and service area the same everywhere?
6. **Crawler access.** Does your site's robots.txt allow AI search crawlers such as OAI-SearchBot? OpenAI explains its crawlers in its [bots documentation](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots).
7. **Service pages.** Do you have one clear page per main service that says what you do, who it's for, where you work, and roughly what it costs?
8. **Reviews.** Do you have recent, genuine reviews on Google and at least one other site customers in your industry use?

## What actually moves the needle

**Write down the facts.** AI systems prefer pages that state things plainly: "We install and repair residential HVAC systems in Charlotte, Matthews, and Mint Hill. Service calls start at $X." Vague marketing copy gives them nothing to quote.

**Answer real questions.** Add an FAQ to each service page using the exact questions customers ask on the phone.

**Add structured data.** Schema markup (LocalBusiness, Service, FAQPage) labels your information so machines can read it reliably. Google's [structured data introduction](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/intro-structured-data) is a good primer.

**Publish an llms.txt file.** This emerging convention, described at [llmstxt.org](https://llmstxt.org/), gives AI tools a short, clean summary of your site and its most important pages.

**Be cited elsewhere.** Local news, chamber of commerce directories, industry associations, and partner websites all count. Assistants trust businesses that other sources mention.

**Keep it fresh.** Update hours, services, and prices when they change, everywhere at once.

## What not to do

Skip anything that promises guaranteed placement in AI answers, fake reviews, or keyword-stuffed pages. Assistants change their answers constantly, and tricks that fool one system tend to backfire.

## Where to start

If you only do three things this month: claim and complete your Google Business Profile, claim Bing Places, and make sure each of your services has its own clear page. That covers most of what assistants look for.

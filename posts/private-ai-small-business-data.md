Many owners hold back on AI for one reason: they don't want customer files, financials, or internal documents going into a public chatbot. That's a sensible instinct. The good news is that you have more options than "paste everything into ChatGPT" or "avoid AI entirely."

## Three levels of AI privacy

**1. Consumer AI tools.** Free and personal plans are convenient, but settings vary, and employees using personal accounts means the business has no control or record. Fine for public information, risky for anything sensitive.

**2. Business-tier AI tools.** Team and enterprise plans from the major providers generally offer admin controls and commitments about not training on your business data. For most small businesses, this is the right starting point. Read each provider's current data terms before you rely on them.

**3. Privately hosted LLMs.** An open-weight large language model runs on hardware you own or in a private cloud account you control. Your prompts and documents never go to a third-party AI provider.

## When a private LLM makes sense

- You handle confidential client files, contracts, or personal information.
- Your industry has data rules, or clients ask hard questions about where data goes.
- You want AI to work across all your internal documents, not just what someone pastes in.
- Per-user subscription costs are adding up across a growing team.

## The honest trade-offs

- **Setup effort.** Someone has to choose the model, set up the hardware or cloud account, connect your documents, and keep it updated.
- **Running cost.** A capable local machine or cloud GPU costs money. It can still be cheaper than many subscriptions, but it isn't free.
- **Capability.** Today's open-weight models handle everyday business tasks well: drafting, summarizing, and answering questions from your documents. The very largest commercial models still lead on the hardest reasoning tasks.
- **Responsibility.** You (or your provider) own security, backups, and access control.

## Three realistic use cases

**An internal knowledge assistant.** Load your employee handbook, procedures, price lists, and past proposals. Staff ask questions in plain English ("What's our policy on rush orders?") and get answers with a link to the source document.

**Document drafting.** Generate first drafts of proposals, scopes of work, and letters from your own templates and past examples, without sending client details outside.

**Inquiry triage.** Read incoming emails or form submissions, classify them, pull out key details, and route them to the right person, with a suggested reply for a human to approve.

## Questions to ask before choosing any AI tool

1. Where is our data stored, and who can access it?
2. Is our data used to train the provider's models? Can we turn that off?
3. Can we see who used the tool and what they did?
4. What happens to our data if we cancel?
5. Which tasks will this actually speed up, and how will we measure it?

## Start small

You don't need to decide everything up front. A common path is to roll out a business-tier tool with a clear [AI use policy](/blog/employee-ai-use-policy/), learn which tasks benefit most, and then move the sensitive workloads to a private model.

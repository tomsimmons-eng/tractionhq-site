Getting your business ready to sell: processes, KPIs and systems

By Traction Consulting · November 15, 2026 · 2 min read

Most owners think about selling their business years before they do it, and then prepare in the final few months. The businesses that sell most smoothly tend to look the same: they run without the owner in every decision, their numbers are easy to understand, and their systems will keep working after the handoff.

Even if you never plan to sell, that same work makes the business less stressful to run.

Why buyers care about operations

A buyer is paying for future results. Anything that makes those results uncertain tends to reduce what they'll pay or how they'll structure the deal. Common worries include:

  • Owner dependence. If key relationships, pricing knowledge, or problem-solving live only in the owner's head, results may walk out the door with them.
  • Unclear numbers. If it takes weeks to answer basic questions about revenue by service or customer, a buyer has to guess, and they'll guess conservatively.
  • Fragile systems. Critical spreadsheets one person understands, software nobody owns, and passwords on sticky notes all signal risk.

Step 1: Document how the business runs

Start with the workflows that bring in money: winning work, delivering it, and getting paid. For each one, write down who does what, in which tool, and what "done" looks like. Simple process maps and checklists are enough.

This is also where AI helps. Record a walkthrough of how you do a task and have AI turn it into a draft procedure your team can refine.

Step 2: Choose KPIs and track them consistently

Pick a short list of numbers that explain your business, and track them the same way every month. Depending on your industry, that might include:

  • Revenue and gross margin by service line
  • Customer concentration (share of revenue from your largest customers)
  • Recurring or repeat revenue
  • Lead volume, conversion rate, and average job value
  • Backlog or pipeline

Consistency matters more than sophistication. Two years of clean monthly numbers tell a stronger story than a fancy dashboard built last month.

Step 3: Modernize and own your systems

  • Put company information in company-owned accounts, not personal ones.
  • Replace fragile spreadsheets with proper tools where it makes sense, and connect them so data isn't typed twice.
  • Keep a simple list of every system, what it does, what it costs, and who has access.
  • Use a password manager and remove access for former employees.

Step 4: Move routine work off the owner

Delegate day-to-day decisions using the processes from step 1, supported by tools and training. Track how many hours a week the owner spends on routine work, and work that number down.

Step 5: Bring in the right professionals

Valuation, tax planning, deal structure, and legal work belong with a qualified business broker, CPA, and attorney. Operational preparation makes their job easier, and it gives you better information to work with when you talk to them.

How long it takes

Ideally, start a year or more before you want to sell so improvements show up in your results. But every month of better documentation and cleaner reporting helps.
